When I set out to create **Bushlandia: The Magic of Imagination in the Australian Bush**, I knew the illustrations would matter as much as the words. Children don't simply read a picture-rich book, they live inside it, so every character, every leaf and every small expression had to feel right. Which brings me to **Braia Matrix**.

## Finding the Right Collaborator

Braia came to me as a freelancer through an online platform. He is based overseas, which sounds like a complication and mostly wasn't. Working across opposite time zones meant we were forever passing the baton: I would wake up to new sketches, send my thoughts back over breakfast, and he would pick them up as his own day began. Because we could never lean over the same desk, every exchange had to be deliberate, and I think the work was better for it.

## The Research Process

What impressed me most was how seriously Braia took the setting. He isn't Australian, and he could easily have drawn a vague, generic forest. Instead he went away and researched the bush properly: the lyrebird at the heart of the story, the native plants, the way light comes down through eucalyptus trees. Australian readers can tell when the bush in a book is really the bush. I wanted them to feel at home on these pages, and thanks to Braia's homework, they do.

## Crafting the Visual Language

The colour palette was one of the biggest decisions we made together. Colour sets the mood of a picture book before a single word is read, and I had strong feelings about what Bushlandia should feel like: warm, inviting, and unmistakably Australian. Braia never tried to impose his own vision over mine. He asked what I was drawn to, paid attention to the answers, and folded my preferences into every frame.

Character design worked the same way. We talked at length about who these children were and how they should feel to a young reader. I shared the styles of art that warmed my heart, he suggested ideas I would never have thought of, and we went back and forth on expressions until each one sat right. Even the small characters in the background ended up with personality and a job to do.

## A Unique Artistic Vision

Braia pairs technical skill with genuine emotional sensitivity. Every line and shadow in the book serves the story, and the style works on two levels at once, for children meeting Bushlandia for the first time and for the adults reading over their shoulders. He never treated this as a commission to get through. He treated it as something to be proud of, and it shows on every page.

## The Power of Visual Storytelling

Illustrations do work that words can't. I can write that the lyrebird is curious, but when a child sees the tilt of its head in Braia's artwork, the bird becomes real to them. The wonder in this book travels through the pictures at least as much as through the text, and that is exactly how a picture book should be.

## A Collaboration Worth Celebrating

If making Bushlandia taught me anything about creative work, it is that the right partner matters far more than proximity. Braia and I have never been in the same room, and it has not once held us back.

To Braia:

> Thank you for seeing the vision of Bushlandia, for bringing the Australian bush to life with such care and artistry, and for making every page of this book something we can all be proud of. Your illustrations aren't just part of Bushlandia; they are Bushlandia.

If you are working on your own illustrated project, my advice is to hold out for a collaborator who shares your vision and brings talents you don't have. Sometimes they find you from the other side of the world.

Braia and I are already busy on the next chapter together. Bushlandia 2 is in creation as I write this.
