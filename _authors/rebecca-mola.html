Rebecca Mola is an Australian children's book author. She writes stories where the fun does the teaching: bush adventures that quietly become lessons in looking after nature, and counting practice disguised as puppy chaos.

Rebecca writes around the edges of ordinary life, which also happens to be where most of her material comes from. Her books celebrate childhood as she thinks it ought to be: outdoors, curious, slightly muddy, and full of made-up worlds. They are written to be read together, and to survive being read for the fortieth time.

Her first two books sit at opposite ends of childhood. Bushlandia is an adventure for readers aged 7 to 10, about four children who build their own town in the bush and have to work out what they owe it. One Puppy, Two Puppies, Hey What Do You Do? counts puppies from one to ten for toddlers who cannot sit still, and never asks them to.

"Every child already has a wild imagination. My job is to feed it, and to sneak in a love of reading while nobody is looking." - Rebecca Mola

Writing Philosophy

Ask Rebecca what her books are for and you will get a short answer. Learning should feel like an adventure, never like homework. Children should know and love the natural world, especially the Australian corner of it. And a good story can carry big things lightly: empathy, teamwork, and the knack of solving problems with your friends.

When She's Not Writing

You will usually find her somewhere in the Australian bush, or in the company of animals, or both at once. She maintains that the best story ideas come from paying attention to the world the way a seven-year-old does: close up, and in no particular hurry.
